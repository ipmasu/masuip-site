XPeng's Humanoid Robot Bet: How Should Automakers Change Their Patent Strategy?

A short IP essay on automakers moving into humanoid robots and transferring autonomy, control, battery, and software patents.

By Ma Su Team2026-08-28Robotics & Patent Layout

Chinese version

XPeng reportedly plans to mass-produce its Iron humanoid robot and expects the robotics business to become more profitable than its car business. The IP significance is clear: when an automaker enters robotics, it does not start from zero. It transfers autonomous driving, batteries, sensors, domain controllers, software updates, and supply-chain capability into embodied AI.

Automotive patents and robotics patents overlap heavily. Perception, path planning, and obstacle avoidance from autonomous driving can migrate to robot navigation. Battery management, thermal design, and lightweight structures from EVs can migrate to robot endurance. OTA systems and data loops can migrate to robot skill updates.

But humanoid robots also create IP problems that cars do not. They need joint actuators, dexterous hands, foot-force control, fall recovery, tactile skin, human-robot interaction, and task learning. If an automaker simply moves its vehicle patent portfolio across, it may leave gaps in end effectors, ergonomics, and service scenarios.

A robotics portfolio for automakers should have three layers: reused automotive technology, robot-body and actuator patents, and scenario patents such as retail assistance, factory handling, home support, and inspection. The real barrier is not the humanoid shape. It is a manufacturable, maintainable, upgradable technical loop.
